# AI Pattern Dictionary

Use this dictionary to diagnose clusters of generic model-like writing. A single
word or structure is not proof that text was AI-generated. Judge repetition,
context, audience, and voice.

## Priority 1: Rewrite By Default

These phrases often add performance without meaning:

- `delve into`, `embark on`, `navigate the landscape`, `in today's fast-paced`
- `unlock`, `unleash`, `elevate`, `supercharge`, `revolutionize`
- `game-changer`, `paradigm shift`, `transformative journey`
- `pivotal`, `crucial`, `testament to`, `underscores the importance`
- `tapestry`, `realm`, `landscape`, `ecosystem` when used as vague metaphor
- `it is worth noting`, `it is important to remember`, `needless to say`
- `here's the thing`, `the result?`, `the key takeaway`, `in conclusion`

Prefer a plain claim, concrete action, or deletion. Do not swap one inflated word
for another.

## Priority 2: Inspect In Context

These words can be correct, but clusters make prose sound templated:

- `robust`, `seamless`, `comprehensive`, `holistic`, `dynamic`, `innovative`
- `leverage`, `utilize`, `facilitate`, `foster`, `empower`, `enable`
- `insights`, `solutions`, `capabilities`, `offerings`, `stakeholders`
- `moreover`, `furthermore`, `additionally`, `consequently`, `ultimately`
- `remarkable`, `significant`, `meaningful`, `valuable`, `impactful`

Keep them only when they are the most accurate words for the audience. Otherwise
name the actual quality, action, person, or result.

## Structural Tells

### Formulaic Contrast

Pattern: `Not X, but Y`, `It is not just X; it is Y`, or repeated `Whether X or Y`.

Rewrite the positive claim directly unless the contrast carries necessary
meaning.

### Automatic Groups Of Three

Pattern: three adjectives, benefits, clauses, or examples used to sound complete.

Keep all three when the source truly defines a three-part set. Otherwise retain
only what matters or rewrite as a natural phrase.

### Rhetorical Question Followed By Its Answer

Pattern: `What does this mean? It means ...`

Lead with the answer. Keep the question only when it reflects a genuine question
in the writer's voice.

### Mirrored Sentences

Pattern: consecutive sentences with identical syntax or length.

Change the angle, sentence length, or relationship between the ideas. Do not
break parallelism that is intentional and useful.

### Dramatic Reveal

Pattern: `Here's the truth:`, `The surprising part?`, `And then it happened.`

Start with the substance unless the source voice genuinely tells stories this
way.

### Inflated Significance

Pattern: a factual sentence followed by a sentence explaining that the fact is
important, historic, or symbolic without evidence.

Delete the editorial inflation. Let the evidence carry the weight.

### Neat Ending On Every Paragraph

Pattern: each paragraph restates its point or ends with a miniature lesson.

Remove redundant wrap-ups. Some paragraphs can stop after the concrete detail,
decision, or unresolved thought.

### Uniform Rhythm

Pattern: similar sentence lengths, predictable transitions, and evenly sized
paragraphs throughout.

Vary pace according to meaning. Do not manufacture variation with random
fragments or punctuation.

### Em-Dash Dependence

Pattern: repeated em dashes doing the work of commas, parentheses, colons, and
sentence breaks.

Choose punctuation by relationship. A rare purposeful em dash is fine.

### Abstract Noun Chains

Pattern: `the implementation of the optimization of ...`

Restore an actor and a verb: name who did what and what changed.

### Empty Reader Guidance

Pattern: `As we have seen`, `This highlights`, `It is clear that`.

Delete it or replace it with the evidence the reader actually needs.

## Secondary Convergence

After editing, scan for a new repeated habit:

- every paragraph starts with `But`, `And`, or `So`;
- every transition becomes `That said` or `The thing is`;
- every sentence becomes short and punchy;
- every ending becomes deliberately unresolved;
- specific detail is inserted where the source had none;
- contractions, slang, fragments, or parentheses appear as a costume.

Human texture comes from the writer's intent and voice, not from satisfying a
fixed count.

## Safe Replacement Method

For each suspected pattern:

1. State the sentence's actual job.
2. Keep its facts, uncertainty, relationship, and emotional force.
3. Remove language that performs importance or completeness.
4. Rewrite from the job rather than editing word by word.
5. Read the surrounding paragraph and remove any new repetition.
